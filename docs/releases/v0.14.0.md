# Open Model Gateway v0.14.0

A minor release after [v0.13.0](v0.13.0.md). It adds access review campaigns (#90). A Platform Admin starts a one-shot review of who has access to Teams and Projects and who holds the Admin and Auditor roles. Each workspace's owners and admins, or the Platform Admins for platform roles, keep, remove or flag each item, and a removal applies at once through the same paths as removing the access directly. **A reviewer's removal is real and immediate:** removing a person's last platform role suspends them and, unless they are reinstated within 30 days, removes their personal data (as revoking the role directly does). It is a minor release because it adds four migrations (**0076**–**0079**), runtime-grant changes, nine management operations, a new kind of item in the notification feed and twelve new `409` reasons in the shared error contract's closed `reason` enum.

These notes do not establish completed release qualification, authenticated/provider acceptance or authorization for production activation or v1.0.0.

## New features

- **Access review campaigns** (#90). Platform Admins start a review from Admin › **Access reviews**, or with `POST /api/v1/platform/access-reviews`. It covers memberships of Teams and Projects (optionally only listed ones) and/or the Admin and Auditor platform roles, and is due 1 to 90 days later (default 14).
  - **What it reviews:** starting a review takes a snapshot of the live grants it covers:
    - manual and group memberships in enabled Teams and Projects (never a personal workspace);
    - Admin and Auditor role grants (the User entitlement every person holds is not reviewed);
    - only for people who are neither disabled nor cleaned up.

    Later grants are not added. An item stays listed as no longer in effect once its grant is revoked, or its person or workspace disabled (or cleaned up). If a suspended person is reactivated, that review does not review their access again.
  - **Bounds:** at most 20 open reviews and 5,000 items per review; a workspace filter lists at most 50 Teams or Projects, and a name is up to 120 characters. Narrow a large review by workspace. A review can be closed, once.
  - **Reading:** Platform Admins and Auditors see each review's decision counts and items, and can export them as CSV (**Export CSV**; `GET /api/v1/platform/access-reviews/{id}/export`, audited).
- **Reviewing** (Workspace sidebar › **Review access**; `GET /api/v1/me/access-reviews`).
  - **Who reviews:** each person sees what they may decide now:
    - the memberships of the Teams and Projects they own or administer;
    - the platform roles, if they are a Platform Admin;
    - anything a Platform Admin assigned to them.

    Nobody reviews their own access, and nobody chooses the reviewer of their own access. The rule is derived live, so losing the role removes the item from the list at once. An assignee who no longer qualifies is ignored and the automatic reviewers decide again.
  - **The decisions:**
    - **Keep.**
    - **Flag for identity provider:** group-provided access is fixed at the identity provider, never revoked here.
    - **Remove:** manual or bootstrap access is removed at once through the same path and protections as removing it directly. If that path refuses (for example the last workspace owner), nothing is recorded.
  - **Removal details:**
    - **A membership** is removed as `DELETE /workspaces/{ws}/members/{user}` does. The manual grant is revoked, and the person's keys there are revoked once no membership remains. An owner is removed only by an owner of that workspace or a Platform Admin.
    - **A platform role** removes only that grant (a group-provided grant of the same role stays). The last Platform Admin and any workspace's last owner are protected. **If the person is left with no platform role, they are suspended at once: their sessions and keys are revoked (revoked keys never return) and their personal data is removed automatically after 30 days unless they are reinstated before then.** This is the same as revoking the role directly; the dashboard's confirmation says so.
    - Membership removals are irreversible too: the revoked keys never return.
  - **Records:** decisions are append-only and audited. While the review is open a reviewer may change a Keep or a Flag; the latest counts, and repeating one's own latest decision records nothing. **A Remove cannot be changed or undone:** it has already revoked the grant.
  - **Who reviews what:** a Platform Admin can assign an item to one eligible reviewer and see which items have **No reviewer** (such as a sole owner's own membership).
- **Reminders** (#90). Reviewers get an in-app notification for each open review in which they still have something to decide:
  - when it opens;
  - again within 24 hours of its due date;
  - again once overdue.

  It disappears once every item they may decide has a decision (by anyone), the items are assigned to someone else, or the review closes. **Open review** goes to Review access. Never emailed or sent to webhooks.

## Behaviour changes

- **Management API:** nine new operations (**272**): six under `/platform/access-reviews` (list, start, read, items, close, assign a reviewer), the export (`/platform/access-reviews/{id}/export`), and two under `/me/access-reviews` (the inbox and decisions, for any entitled person). Platform Admins start, close and assign; Platform Admins and Auditors read and export. All are browser-session only; every admin token is refused.
- **`GET /api/v1/me/notifications`:** items can be access review reminders (`source: "access_review"`, with the review's id, `state` `open`, `due_soon` or `overdue`, name, due date and read state). The summary's `unread` counts them, and `POST /me/notifications/read` marks them by review id. Clients that assume every item is an alert incident or an access request need the new contract.
- **New `409` reasons:** `access_review_limit`, `access_review_too_large`, `access_review_empty`, `access_review_changed`, `access_review_closed`, `access_review_item_inactive`, `access_review_flag_requires_group`, `access_review_reviewer_ineligible`, `access_review_own_access`, `access_review_remove_requires_manual`, `access_review_owner_removal` and `access_review_removal_refused`.
- **Audit:**
  - **New actions:** `access_review.created`, `access_review.closed`, `access_review.exported` (resource `access_review`), `access_review.decided` (with the decision) and `access_review.reviewer_assigned` (`assigned` or `cleared`) (resource `access_review_item`; in the item's workspace audit for membership items, installation-level for platform-role items).
  - **On removal:** a removal also records the path's own event (`workspace.manual_membership_revoked` or `role.revoked`).
- **Admission:** a review's removal of a platform role takes the exclusive catalog lock, as revoking the role directly does, so it briefly holds back new admissions.
- **Refactor:** the direct removal routes (`DELETE /workspaces/{ws}/members/{user}`, `DELETE /platform/workspaces/{ws}/members/{user}` and `DELETE /platform/users/{user}/roles/{role}`) now share their implementation with review removals. Their behaviour is unchanged.
- **Personal data:**
  - Review names are immutable operator labels: never put personal data in them.
  - Reviews record people by user id. Their names and emails are shown as the platform user directory shows them now, so they are empty once removed.
  - Platform Admins and Auditors newly see, per review, who was reviewed, their role and workspace, and who decided or was assigned each item. A review's CSV export contains the people under review (display name and email), workspace names, and the deciders' and reviewers' emails: nothing beyond what they already see in the user directory and workspace lists. Every reviewer sees the review's name in reminders.
  - **Retention:** review, item, decision, assignment and read-mark rows are kept indefinitely and keyed by user id. They are **not** included in the subject exports (`/me/export`, the platform user export); **Remove personal data** clears the person's name and email on the user row but leaves these rows, so the role, workspace and who decided remain, by id, as in the audit log.

## Migrations and runtime grants

- **0076_access_review_campaigns:**
  - **`access_review_campaigns`:** started by a current Platform Admin on the server clock, due 1 to 90 days out, with a non-blank name without control characters. Only closing changes it, once, by a current Platform Admin.
  - **`access_review_items`:** an item names its grant, and the database copies the workspace, person, role, source and grant time from it. Items are accepted only in the transaction that started their review, while it is open, and never change.
- **0077_access_review_decisions:**
  - the append-only `access_review_assignments` and `access_review_decisions`, whose guards set the server clock and a per-item order and apply the reviewer rule;
  - the functions `access_review_item_active`, `access_review_candidate` and `access_review_may_decide`;
  - an index on the items' workspace.
- **0078_access_review_removal:**
  - replaces the decision CHECK on the new decisions table and the 0077 guard function, and allows `remove`: manual or bootstrap access only, and an owner's membership only by an owner of the workspace or a Platform Admin;
  - adds a deferred check that refuses, at commit, a `remove` decision whose own grant is not revoked.
- **0079_access_review_notifications:** the insert-only `access_review_notification_reads`, with the server's read time.
- **Runtime grants** (`deploy/staging/runtime-grants.sql`, applied by the chart's migrate Job):
  - `SELECT` on the five tables;
  - column `INSERT` without the server-set columns;
  - `UPDATE(closed_by, closed_at)` on campaigns;
  - `EXECUTE` on the three functions (added to the executable-function allowlist the privilege check enforces).

  Nothing is deleted from any of these tables. The schema preflight (startup and `/health/ready`) inventories them.

## Tests and CI

- **Real PostgreSQL:** 40 access-review tests. They cover:
  - the snapshot and its bounds (5,000 items, 20 open, concurrent starts);
  - the database's binding of items to their review and grant;
  - who reviews what, compared directly with `access_review_may_decide`;
  - assignment and its fallback;
  - live loss of authority;
  - every decision, including removals through both paths with their protections;
  - concurrency: a decision against a close, two removals of one item, a removal against a close or the direct member route, and access changing while a review starts;
  - reminders, the export's content and formula safety;
  - load tests at 20 reviews × 5,000 items for the inbox and the notification poll while items are undecided. The poll's worst case, a reviewer of every item who has decided all of them, is not measured: close finished reviews.
- **Privilege probes:** `verify-privileges.sql` checks the new tables' grants, guards and functions under `gateway_runtime`.
- **Web and browser:** web tests cover the pages, and a browser spec walks starting a review, the owner removing access from Review access, and the Auditor reading it.

## Known limitations

- **Single Platform Admin:** in an installation with one Platform Admin, that admin's own role has no reviewer. Each sole owner's own membership likewise has none unless assigned to a Platform Admin. Both are shown as **No reviewer**.
- **Keys and service accounts** are not review subjects yet (the remaining part of R18). Keys already show a last-used time and expire within 1 to 365 days (human keys also within the installation's maximum lifetime), and Key safety flags stale ones.
- **One-off reviews:** reviews run once, with no recurring schedule, and snapshot once: grants added later are never reviewed. Reminders are in-app only.
- **Due dates only remind:** nothing happens automatically at the due date; overdue access stays until a reviewer removes it. Closing is manual and final.
- **Flag only records:** flagging group access sends nothing to the identity provider, and the access stays until it is changed there. Removing a manual grant does not end access the person holds through a group or another grant.
- **Platform Admins** review platform roles; they decide workspace memberships only when assigned.
- **Lifetime of records:** reviews, decisions and read marks are kept, never deleted; closed reviews cannot be deleted and names cannot be changed. At most 20 reviews are open at a time.

## Upgrade

From **v0.13.0**, replace the image and chart, using the normal drain procedure only after qualification and deployment approval.

1. **Back up.** Take a backup and `verify` it before migrating (`docs/operations.md`, "Upgrade and migration procedure"); the rollback below depends on it.
2. **Drain, then migrate.** A gateway serves only a database whose migrations match its own. v0.14.0 does not start on a v0.13.0 schema. Once 0076 has committed, v0.13.0 does not start and its running replicas report not ready. Drain first.
   - **With the chart:** scale the v0.13.0 Deployment to 0 (and suspend any autoscaler), then `helm upgrade --set migrations.run=true`. The migrate Job is off by default and runs as a pre-upgrade hook.
   - **Without the chart:** run `open-model-gateway migrate` and then `open-model-gateway grants apply` as the migrator.
   - The latest migration is **0079**, with **78 migration records**, and the runtime grants change (above).
   - If the migrate Job fails after a migration has committed, fix the cause and re-run `migrate` and `grants apply` with v0.14.0. Do not `helm rollback`, since v0.13.0 cannot serve that schema.
   - **Locks** (from the SQL, not measured): the migrations create tables, indexes, functions and triggers on new tables. 0078 alters only the new decisions table. Foreign keys briefly take `SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE` on `users`, `workspaces`, `workspace_membership_grants` and `platform_role_grants`. Those locks queue behind open transactions on those tables and block their writers while they wait, so do not migrate while other writers are active (the drain above covers the gateway). No existing table is rewritten or scanned.
3. **Start and verify.** Deploy v0.14.0 by verified image digest. Restore the replica count and any autoscaler you suspended (`helm upgrade` may leave a manually scaled Deployment at 0). Wait for `/health/ready` to report `schema: ok`, run `open-model-gateway grants verify`, then check sign-in and one bounded inference request.
4. **Do not** reset or recreate an existing database, run a down migration or bootstrap implicitly.
5. **Rollback.** There are no down migrations. Redeploying v0.13.0 is only safe if no migration ran; otherwise restore the pre-upgrade backup into a new database and cut over. Never run old workers against the new schema.
6. **Clients.** Regenerate generated management clients: there are nine new operations, a new notification item shape and twelve new error reasons in a closed enum.

From an older release, also follow the [v0.13.0 upgrade instructions](v0.13.0.md#upgrade) and their linked requirements. Publication verification and readiness remain separate from production activation and authenticated/provider acceptance.
